# What's modeled

## Rendering

The game uses Three.js and procedural geometry. Kirov has a shaped hull, stepped superstructure, weapons, radar masts, deck fittings and a first-person bridge. The bridge's windows look onto the same ocean and ship used by the exterior camera. Original Kirov photographs inform its materials and fittings; see [the reference index](../refs/README.md).

The Gerstner-wave ocean, foam/wakes, physical atmosphere and shared shader utilities originate in Battle Stations. Wave sampling gives the ship heave, pitch and roll. The ocean follows the camera; it is not a physics boundary for the combat simulation. Radar antennas rotate, crew members shift and look around, and the Soviet naval ensign moves in the wind.

Drumfire's post-processing supplies a shared-depth HDR pipeline with ambient occlusion, eye adaptation, bloom, color grading and explosion shockwaves. Echelon's effects supply lit, sorted missile smoke, motor plumes, fireballs, sparks and debris. Destroyed ships list and sink; aircraft fall trailing smoke.

Day, dusk, heavy-weather and night presets change lighting, cloud cover and sea conditions. Heavy weather is a presentation preset, not a complete meteorological or sea-state model. Low, Medium and High quality settings trade rendering resolution, shadows, ambient occlusion, bloom and effect budget. These are selectable presets, not a claim of measured frame rate on every device.

## Simulation

The simulation runs in fixed 0.05-second steps and can run without a browser. Its design adapts Echelon's separation of simulation and rendering, engagement envelopes and closest-approach interception. It uses a seeded random generator.

- **Ship handling:** course changes and acceleration take time. Propulsion damage limits speed.
- **Sensors:** contacts depend on radar state, range, sweep updates and a simplified radar horizon. Reconnaissance supplies initial surface intelligence.
- **Weapons:** finite magazines, launch intervals, moving launch positions, minimum/maximum range, altitude gates, missile acceleration and turn limits.
- **Guidance:** Fort and Osa depend on continuing radar support; prolonged guidance loss terminates the engagement. Granit and Bazalt leave along the launcher on their boosters, then turn under a g limit, cruise low (or high on long shots) and run in at sea-skimming height; `ASM` in `src/sim.js` separates the documented figures from the estimates. Granit rounds rippled at one target fly as a salvo: the pack runs in low while one round, the scout, climbs out of it in short pop-ups (to about 550 m every 15 s) and drops back. If the scout is lost, the next round with room rises at once to take over. A lone round flies low. Open sources report the scout, its pop-ups and the takeover, and some doubt them; the heights and timing are estimates.
- **Defense:** automatic crews select threats; Fort and Osa have limited target channels; AK-630 uses short-range bursts and ammunition. Chaff can divert some incoming seekers.
- **Damage:** hull integrity and radar, propulsion and weapons condition are simplified game state, not structural or blast calculations.

The 13-minute scenario combines two surface opponents, aircraft attacks and staged incoming missile waves. Some waves represent weapons already airborne from forces outside the rendered encounter. They can arrive after the visible launching platforms have been destroyed. This ensures the initial session includes all three threat types; it is an authored encounter, not a full fleet operational simulation.

## Accuracy boundary

Public platform and weapon characteristics establish recognizable capabilities. Engagement timing, hit chances, chaff success, damage values and enemy behavior are game rules. AK-100 and antisubmarine fittings are visual detail in this version. There is no nuclear weapon employment, submarine warfare, multiplayer, full bridge walk-through or reactor-control simulation.

## Sound

Six bundled CC0 recordings layer over WebAudio synthesis. A crossfaded bow-wave recording follows ship speed; wind and low machinery harmonics continue underneath. On the bridge, exterior sounds are filtered through the enclosure while machinery and short room reflections become more prominent.

Launches, impacts and CIWS bursts use positional sound. Distant explosions arrive after acoustic travel time and lose high frequencies with range. The mix caps active voices and queued effects, uses a compressor, and keeps synthesized fallbacks for unavailable recordings. Damage adds metal resonance and a warning alarm. Chaff and interface actions have separate cues. Optional browser speech is disabled by default. These are sound-design reconstructions, not authentic recordings of these Soviet weapons. [Recording credits](../public/audio/CREDITS.md).

## Opposing crews

Frigates zigzag and maneuver when Granits approach, with finite chaff releases and short-range defensive bursts. Each also has four area SAMs for a Granit salvo's scout while it is popped up; a scout already back among the sea-skimmers when the SAM arrives escapes it. Those defensive opportunities use authored probabilities; they are not a complete Perry-class radar, Standard missile or CIWS simulation. Aircraft bank through their turns. Seeded variations alter approach bearings and ranges, so a salvo plan should be followed by assessment rather than assuming every missile will hit.

## Crew

The bridge and deck crew share one skinned figure module, `src/models/kirov-crew.js`, ported from Drumfire's crew rig. Each figure is one skinned mesh and one draw call with rigid skinning, 4.0k to 5.0k triangles, geometry shared per outfit and a single shared material. Poses cross-fade, and two-bone IK keeps hands on moving controls: the helmsman's two vertical grips, the telegraph lever and console edges. Idle motion (breathing, weight shift, head turns and occasional glances) runs with a per-figure phase, so a group never moves in lockstep. `fig.brace()` makes a figure brace for a hit and then return to its station.

Dress follows the Soviet Navy's Northern Fleet at sea in autumn 1985:

- **Officers:** the large naval furazhka with black top, gold chin cord and the gold "krab" badge; the black double-breasted tuzhurka over a white shirt and black tie; gold sleeve rings; shoulder boards. The officer of the watch wears the black officer's jacket with a fur collar and carries binoculars.
- **Ratings:** the blue working dress tucked in under the black belt with its brass anchor buckle; the telnyashka at the V neck; a battle number patch; the bezkozyrka with white piping, star cockade, a ribbon lettered "СЕВЕРНЫЙ ФЛОТ" and two tails; the black pilotka; the flannel jumper with the blue guyus collar.
- **Deck watches:** the black bushlat with 2 x 5 gold buttons, shoulder boards with the fleet letters, and the black ushanka with its flaps tied up; gloves; the orange life jacket for upper-deck crew; lookouts with 7x50 binoculars; talkers with a headset and a chest microphone.

Faces have modelled noses, brows, chins and ears, plus a procedural face texture (eye sockets, irises, lash lines, brows, nostrils, lips, a seed-chosen moustache) so they read at bridge distance. The ribbon lettering and battle number come from the same small procedural atlas; no photographs are used as textures. Summer whites, rank-accurate insignia for every grade and individual uniform wear are not modelled. `preview/crew.html` shows every outfit and pose.

## Weapons

Kirov's 1985 armament is modelled as working machinery in `src/models/kirov-weapons.js` and `src/models/weapons/`, placed from the exterior's `MOUNTS` table. Repeated launchers and guns are instanced: the whole fit draws in about 95 calls and 160k triangles. Mechanisms are driven by state each frame. The fire-control order, the automatic defence's posture and the rounds in flight decide what each launcher, gun and director should be doing. Each mechanism then moves toward that at its own rate. Every step of the manual order shows on the ship, and the launcher-inspection camera (`4`) follows it.

Under automatic defence the same machinery follows the crew's posture. Directors track the nearest air threat in their arcs. The next Fort launcher stands with its hatch open while a threat is inside the envelope. The Osa launchers stand up while a threat is near. A launch the simulation orders before a mechanism is ready snaps that launcher open. Rounds leave from their actual cell or rail along its axis (a model hook in the simulation, `setLaunchFrames`). They keep the ship's velocity while they clear the launcher. The flying round is the same model as the one in the cell: P-700 wings and 5V55 fins unfold after launch.

What is documented and what is reconstructed (sources in [the reference index](../refs/README.md#weapons)):

- **Documented:** the counts and layouts (20 Granit in 4 by 5, 12 Fort drums of 8 as two complexes of six, two ZIF-122 with 20 rounds each, eight AK-630 in four pairs, two AK-100, two 3R41). - **Read from photographs:** the Granit lids' shape, plinth and starboard hinge arms, and the Fort hatches' pyramid lids and frames (Frunze, 1985). The Top Dome's drum, saddle and link antennas and the 4R33's tan twin radomes (Frunze, 1985). The AK-100 gunhouse (Kirov, 1986).
- **Reconstructed:** the Granit tube inclination (60 degrees; one source gives 47), wells, water level and lid travel. The Fort hatch hinge, drum size and where the drum sits under its hatch. The Osa door, column and arm proportions. Most mount dimensions not listed above, the MR-184 and 4R33 heads in detail, lamp colours and every timing. Fire-control step durations are game timings.
- **Presentation only:** the AK-100s have no simulated engagement, so their firing does nothing in the simulation. Metel and the RBUs never train.

`preview/weapons.html` shows the weapons alone or on the ship (`?ship=1`) and steps each through its sequence by hand or by URL. `tools/weapons-game.mjs` captures each weapon's sequence in the game.

## Exterior

The exterior (`src/models/kirov-exterior.js` and `src/models/exterior/`) is a procedural reconstruction of Kirov as photographed by the US Navy in 1983 to 1986, built with Drumfire's modelling kit: geometry is collected per material and merged, and fittings sit in detail tiers that drop out with distance.

**Measured from photographs.** The near-orthographic port-beam photograph of 26 October 1983 (about 10 px per metre over the whole ship) and a closer frame of the same pass (about 23 px per metre) give the longitudinal layout and the heights. That is: where each block, tower, mast, weapon group and deck break stands; the sheer; the knuckle line; and the bridge roofline at about 23 m. Cameras were then solved to match the anchored 1985 bow photograph (5 points, 12 px rms on a 3000 px frame) and the 1986 stern quarter (6 points, about 50 px rms). Renders from those cameras were overlaid on the photographs to correct the stem rake, freeboard, hull number, hawse pockets, the forward Top Dome, the aft gun platforms and the flight deck. `src/models/exterior/lines.js` records each measured value and how sure it is.

**What it shows.**

- **Hull:** a lofted hull with the hard knuckle running aft from the stem and stepping down abaft the hull number. Forecastle, main deck and quarterdeck follow the stepped sheer. Also: the raked stem and stem bar, hawse pockets with stockless anchors and chain, the broad transom with its variable-depth-sonar doors, portholes with rims and eyebrows, scuppers, and torpedo-tube shutters aft. The red star on each bow, hull number 065, and КИРОВ on the quarters are drawn decals.
- **Superstructure:** the stepped forward block, with the 01-level side screens and their oval openings and the 02 and 03 levels with raked fronts. The navigation bridge face matches the bridge module's room, with the wings and their spray screens. The forward Top Dome house sits on the bridge roof.
- **Forward tower:** mast and uptake in one tapering pyramid, with the slot to the separate after uptake trunk. It carries galleries, the cap platform with its ribbed drums, Side Globe EW radomes on brackets, and the lattice yard with two rotating Palm Fronds. The conical mast carries the rotating MR-800 Top Pair: a curved lattice reflector with a shaped outline, rim tube, curved back ribs, girders and bracing, and a horizontal line feed held low in front. Back to back with it is the oval lattice dish with its ribs and a horn on a boom. Both stand on a turntable with a gear ring, rotary joint and a box-girder boom. Top Steer is built the same way at a smaller size.
- **Surface clutter:** every superstructure and tower face carries weld seams and plate butts, cable trays with drops to junction boxes, small equipment housings on brackets, pipe runs with flanges and valves, recessed doorways with jambs and dark reveals, and antenna brackets high up. Grey rain streaks hang under roof edges. Wall bases are darkened where they meet the deck, and the upper forward tower sits in the cap's shadow.
- **Windows:** framed panes set back in frames proud of the wall, with rain visors. The glass carries a texture of the dark room behind with a sky reflection, plus a sharp environment reflection; portholes have heavy rims.
- **Amidships and aft:** the main-deck galleries with large arched openings holding rows of PSN liferaft canisters (1985 bow photograph). Their roof is the boat deck, where white cabin launches and canvas-covered workboats sit in cradles with gripes. Two pedestal cranes on the aft block have box jibs with lightening holes, stowed rising forward over the boat bays as photographed in 1983 and 1985. The four Bass Tilt radomes stand on columns; the midships pair is staggered, port at z 4.3 and starboard at z 14.5, as the two photographs show. The aft block carries its tower with the rotating MR-700 Top Steer, the aft lattice mast and the aft Top Dome house. The AK-100 barbettes have splinter screens, and the aft AK-630 platforms stand on the quarterdeck.
- **Deck fittings:** railings with stanchions and three courses, watertight doors with dogs, louvres, mushroom and gooseneck vents, ladders with cages, PSN liferaft racks, lockers, life rings, windlasses, capstans, bollards and fairleads. The helicopter deck has markings, the elevator grid, guide rails and folded safety nets. Whips and halyards, the naval ensign on the gaff (animated) and nine deck crew from `kirov-crew.js` complete the fit: lookouts on the bridge wings and tower platforms, a watch officer, a signalman and deckhands.
- **Paint:** procedural welded-plate textures, with butt and seam welds, dishing between frames, rare chips to primer, rust runs and repaint patches. Red-brown non-skid deck paint. A shader keyed to ship-space position adds waterline grime, the white boot top, red antifouling, soot round the uptake mouths and a little salt on up-facing surfaces. Colours were checked against the colour frames of 1983 and 1989. No photograph is used as a texture.

**Reconstructed, not measured.** Plan widths of the blocks seen only from one side, the underwater body, the interiors of the bays, and the exact positions of small fittings (lockers, vents, roof machinery, EW drums, searchlights) are estimates in the style the photographs show. The identities and fits of individual EW antennas are not claimed.

**Deliberate deviations.**

- The two 4R33 directors are placed at the forward block's sides (x ±6 m), where the photographs suggest positions nearer the centreline. This keeps the bridge's view of the Granit and Fort hatches clear.
- The 02 level is narrowed forward of z −22.8, so the Osa bays at the 01-level corners have about 3.5 m of clear space for the launcher's rise and train.
- The DF loop mast in front of the bridge is moved to port for the same reason.
- The forecastle freeboard (13.1 m at the stem) is a compromise between the 1983 frame (13.9 m) and the anchored 1985 photograph (11 to 12 m).
- The Fort hatches are spaced evenly; the photographs show them unevenly and partly hidden.

**Known differences from the photographs.**

- The forward tower's cap is still a broader, plainer platform than the photographed one, which is a stack of smaller platforms, hoods and drums.
- The block faces read lighter and less contrasty than the photographs, because the preview lacks ambient occlusion (the game's post-processing adds it).
- The lattice grids are a texture on a curved surface, not individual bars.
- Boats and cranes match the photographed arrangement and proportions, but their details are reconstructed.
- The transom's sonar doors are simplified.

**Budget.** Measured in the game, without weapons and bridge but with the nine crew figures: 107 meshes and 290k triangles within 420 m, 83 meshes and 179k triangles to 950 m, and 50 meshes and 105k triangles beyond. Each mesh is one draw call, plus one in the shadow pass.

**API.** `buildExterior({ weapons, crew, crewFactory })` returns the group, the rotating radars, the animate callback and the old weapon functions. With `weapons: false` (as the game builds it) the weapon functions are no-ops. `MOUNTS` gives the deck positions of every launcher, gun and director for `kirov-weapons.js`, `deckHeightAt(z)` gives the deck height, and `BRIDGE_ANCHOR` gives the bridge room's origin. `preview/exterior.html` renders the exterior alone, or the whole composed ship with `?full=1`, with cameras matched to the reference photographs (`?view=bow`, `?view=aft`, `?view=port`).

**Third-person camera.** The orbit centre rides on the ship and turns with her heading, smoothed over about 0.7 s. The player's orbit and zoom stay relative to the ship while she steams and manoeuvres. The default view is the starboard bow quarter, about 17 degrees above the horizon, at the distance where the whole ship fills about 90 % of the portrait viewport. Zoom runs from 50 m to 1.6 km.

## Slava exterior

The Slava exterior (`src/models/slava-exterior.js` and `src/models/slava/`) is a procedural reconstruction of the lead ship of Project 1164 as the US Navy photographed her in 1983 to 1986 (hull number 126 in 1983, 108 from 1984; the model carries 108). It is built with the Kirov exterior's kit (`src/models/exterior/kit.js`, `fittings.js`, `clutter.js`, `boats.js`, the Top Pair, Top Steer and Palm Frond builders in `masts.js`, the crew posts in `crew.js`) and its own lines, hull, superstructure, deck and equipment modules. Its materials are Kirov's palette with their own finish uniforms, so both ships can share a scene.

**Measured from photographs.** The near-orthographic port-beam colour frame of 28 January 1986 (about 11.7 px per metre) gives the longitudinal layout and the heights. That is: where each block, mast, launcher pair, gun and deck break stands; the sheer, from 9.95 m at the stem to 6.0 m amidships; the 1.8 m step down to the quarterdeck; the stem and transom profiles; and the bridge level (floor 17.4 m, roof 20.1 m). Cameras were then solved on five more frames, from 8 to 18 correspondences each, with rms errors of 9 to 22 px. On the high frames the camera's height trades against its distance and lens, so the height was held where the decks' visible widths and the horizon (out of frame) put it. These were the 1984 elevated port beam, the 1985 aerial over the bow, the 1983 port bow, the 1983 starboard bow and the 1986 port quarter. On the 1985 frame the Bazalt containers' x positions were solved as unknowns. Rays from the 1984 camera, intersected with the measured deck heights, give the plan widths and the positions across the ship: the containers, the centreline house, the bridge's faceted plan, the Fort plinths, the Osa drums, the hangar lid and the helicopter pad. The Marshal Ustinov aerials of 1988 and photographs of 1989 (same layout) were used for the arrangement seen from above. `src/models/slava/lines.js` records each value and how sure it is.

**What it shows.**

- **Hull:** a lofted hull with a smoothly flared topside and no knuckle. The strong forward sheer runs to a convex, raked stem. Also: the quarterdeck step, and a broad transom with rounded corners raked forward to a foot just clear of the water. Anchors are stowed in pockets high on the bow, with chain to the windlasses. There are portholes in two irregular rows, scuppers, and the torpedo-tube shutters under the aft block. Hull number 108 and СЛАВА on the quarters are drawn decals. The shader adds the white boot top, waterline grime and red antifouling.
- **Forward superstructure:** the narrow centreline house runs from abaft the gun to the main block. It carries the forward AK-630 pair (the after one a deck higher), the RBU-6000 trunks and the forward Bass Tilt on its pedestal. Outboard of it are the four Bazalt support frames on each side: two webs per pair, the outer one with the photographed walk-through opening, a dark recess behind, saddles shaped to the container's belly, and a cross girder to the house. There is a closing fairing abaft the fourth pair.
- **Bridge block and foremast:** the 02 and 03 levels over the containers. The navigation bridge has a faceted plan (an 8.5 m flat front, chamfers, 14.4 m across the sides), and its windowed front leans out 0.8 m at the roof. It has framed panes with wipers and a sun visor, round ports aft, the open wings with spray screens, and the wing doors. The MR-123 radomes for the midships AK-630s sit on brackets under the bridge level. On the roof stands the tall faceted pylon. It carries the Argon "Front Door" aperture in its forward face, galleries at 26 m, the curved lattice dish on its forward port corner, satcom dishes and the rotating MR-700 Top Steer on its pedestal. Abaft it is the lattice mast with its yard, two rotating Palm Fronds, halyards, wire antennas and the ensign on its gaff.
- **Midships:** the main-mast block, with the AK-630 sponsons and liferaft racks on its 01 level. On each side are the four Side Globe radomes (two on outriggers from the block, two on the mast) and two Rum Tub drums on bracketed platforms. The tapering main mast carries the rotating MR-800 Top Pair. The uptake casing follows the 1986 frame: a lower forward section with two large grille frames, the casing proper with two bands of intake grilles, a raked aft face, and the black cap with its brim over the uptake mouths. Abaft it are the boat crane, two motor launches and a workboat in cradles, and the covered 45 mm saluting guns.
- **Aft:** the two Fort plinths, with the covered loading gear between them. They are light grey, as they read from above in 1984; by 1989 they were red-brown. The aft block has windows, doors and liferaft racks. On it are the Top Dome house, a chamfered box with doors and a red-brown foot, and the two Pop Group pedestals. The narrower hangar part has its sloped door, cheeks and stowed boom. Below are the hangar lid between the Osa positions, and the raised helicopter pad on its central housing and edge pillars, with its markings, deck lights, net frames and control cab. On the quarterdeck under the pad are capstans, bollards, lockers and the fire station.
- **Paint:** Kirov's light naval grey and weathering, with the red-brown weather deck of the 1986 colour frame. The superstructure roofs are a light grey non-skid, because they read as light as the walls in the frames from above.
- **Surface detail:** every superstructure wall goes through the same clutter pass as Kirov's: weld seams, cable trays with drops to junction boxes, equipment boxes, pipe runs, recessed doors, antenna brackets and grime streaks. It also carries railings, ladders, louvres, vents, lockers and life rings. Rust streaks are drawn under the anchor pockets and scuppers. Nine deck crew stand at their posts from `kirov-crew.js`: lookouts on the bridge wings and the foremast gallery, a watch officer, a signalman on the bridge roof, and deckhands on the forecastle, by the boats and on the quarterdeck.

**Reconstructed, not measured.** The underwater body and the bow sonar dome; plan widths of blocks seen only obliquely (about plus or minus 0.5 m); interiors; the exact positions of small fittings (lockers, vents, roof machinery, EW drums, searchlights, whips); the hangar's inside; the boat crane's detail.

**Deliberate deviations.**

- The bridge level is built closed with its own glazing by default. Kirov's 15.8 m room does not fit the faceted plan. `buildSlava()` builds the exterior with `bridgeInterior: true`, which opens the level inside `SLAVA_BRIDGE` for the bridge module's Slava-shaped room. The floor slab, the roof slab, the wings and the wing doors stay the exterior's.
- The published 186.4 m length is kept. At high zoom the 1986 frame's hull ends read about 0.9 % shorter, which is within the measurement error.
- The forward Bass Tilt stands 1.7 m to starboard, as on the Marshal Ustinov aerial, clear of the bridge's centre view of the gun.

**Known differences from the photographs.**

- The lattice mast, its yard and the wire aerials are simpler than photographed.
- The boat crane is the Kirov kit's pedestal crane, not the photographed derrick.
- The hangar door and the clutter under the helicopter pad are simplified.
- Block faces read lighter than in the photographs without the game's ambient occlusion.

**Budget.** Measured in `preview/slava.html` on the composed ship (`?full=1&interior=1`, whose header splits the counts by module and by tier). The exterior, with the nine crew figures, has 100 meshes and 240k triangles within 420 m, 80 meshes and 162k triangles to 950 m, and 52 meshes and 95k triangles beyond. Without the crew it is 91 meshes and 197k triangles close up. Each mesh is one draw call, plus one in the shadow pass. The weapons add 79 meshes and 122k triangles, and the bridge room 61 meshes and 280k triangles.

**API.** `buildSlavaExterior({ crew, crewFactory, hullNumber, bridgeInterior })` returns `{ group, radars, animate, crew, stats, MOUNTS, bridge }`. It also exports `SLAVA_MOUNTS`, every weapon and director position for `slava-weapons.js`. The other exports are `slavaDeckHeightAt(z)` (the deck height), `SLAVA_BRIDGE_ANCHOR` (the bridge room's origin, its front face on the photographed bridge front at z -31.8) and `SLAVA_BRIDGE` (the measured bridge level for a Slava room). `preview/slava.html` renders the exterior alone, or the composed ship with `?full=1` (add `&interior=1` for the game's open bridge level). Its cameras are solved on the reference photographs (`?view=bow`, `port`, `elev`, `stern`, `sbow`). `?proxies=1` adds plain stand-ins for the weapons at `SLAVA_MOUNTS`. The photograph comparisons are in `shots/slava/compare-*.png`.

## Bridge

The navigation bridge (`src/models/kirov-bridge.js` and `src/models/bridge/`) is the room behind the forward face of the bridge level. It is built with a kit adapted from Drumfire's: geometry is collected per material and merged, rounded boxes, lathes and profiles are used throughout, and generated textures carry the wear. The fittings follow Oleg Kuleshov's 2015 photographs aboard the ship. Gaps in period style are filled from Soviet ships of the same era (Minsk, Razumny). `refs/bridge-notes.md` separates what the photographs show from what is reconstructed.

- **Room:** pale sea-green bulkheads with screwed battens, seven forward windows and the chamfered corners, a window and a weathertight door to each wing, the wood-trimmed aft bulkhead. Each window has its own deep cast frame, hinged sash, seal, five cream lobed dog knobs, a sloped ledge, heater junction boxes on the pilasters, an eyebrow and a wiper outside. The varnished timber sill runs over a panelled face with heater louvres and red night lamps. The perforated overhead has ribs, dome lamps in square bezels, conduit, hanging rods, diffusers and a loudspeaker. The brown deck covering is worn along the walking paths.
- **Controls:** the curved cast helm with its two chromed grips on a turning bar and a gyro repeater card in its sloped face; the twin-drum telegraph with lettered bands, glazed order dials, levers and two reply dials; voice pipes with bell mouths; the cream optical sight on its column, with a bolted head, knurled wheel, eyepiece and handgrips; two gyro bearing repeaters; the commander's chair; the instrument row over the windows (clinometer, clock, shaft revolutions, rudder angle indicator, log, anemometer).
- **Consoles and wall fittings:** navigation radar indicator, Fort scope, command post console, chart table with chart, instruments and log book, echo sounder and receiver cases, telephones, intercom, extinguishers, signal-watch cabinet and the watch bill.
- **Surface finish:** paint, hammer finish, timber, perforated panel and linoleum textures are generated from tiling noise. A shader adds chipped edges (from per-vertex edge marks on bevels and lathe rims), scuffs and polish in the kick zone, grime at the deck and dust on ledges. Room occlusion (corners, under the sill, round furniture bases) is baked into vertex colours. Dial faces, placards, stencils and the chart share one generated 2048 px atlas.
- **Crew:** six watchkeepers from `kirov-crew.js`. The helmsman keeps both hands on the grips, the telegraph rating holds the lever as it moves, the lookout uses binoculars, the navigator works the chart, and a talker wears a headset. The weapons officer follows the firing sequence at the repeat station.
- **Displays and motion:** the weapons repeat station shows each firing stage on annunciator tiles and a status window, turns its weapon selector and lifts the launch guard once armed. The radar PPI and Fort scope draw live contacts. Telegraph levers, reply pointers, helm bar, compass cards, needles and clock hands follow the game state. Wipers sweep in heavy weather.
- **Lighting:** dome-lamp spots that brighten for dusk and heavy weather, and red night lighting with backlit dials after dark. The room keeps its own lights, so they follow the bridge's position in the ship.
- **View:** the eye stands at the conning position, 3.4 m behind the centre window. Looking down leans it toward the glass so the Granit and Fort hatches show over the sill. Scrolling narrows the view to binoculars.

**Reconstructed:** the room's plan dimensions (the height follows the exterior's photo measurement), the station layout, instrument faces and wording, the night lighting, and four gameplay consoles (radar indicator, Fort scope, command post console, weapons repeat station). The repeat station mirrors the game's firing order; no such fitting is claimed for the real bridge.

**Budget:** 201k triangles and 61 meshes with the six crew figures. That is about 60 draw calls per pass (85 to 115 a frame with the shadow pass), and texture generation adds about 2 s at startup. `preview/bridge.html` renders the bridge alone or in the ship (`?ship=1`), with cameras matched to the reference photographs (`?view=controls|helm|telegraph|sight|chair|outside`) and any firing stage (`?stage=armed`).

**Slava plan.** `buildBridge({ anchor, plan })` builds the same room to another ship's measured bridge. With `plan: SLAVA_BRIDGE` from `slava-exterior.js` it follows the Slava (Project 1164) navigation bridge as the exterior measured it from photographs:

- a 2.7 m level with the floor at ship y 17.4;
- a flat front 8.5 m wide with chamfers out to the 14.4 m sides;
- the front and chamfers raked out 0.8 m at the roof;
- 8 front windows, 4 per chamfer and 5 per side, from sill 18.3 to head 19.3;
- wing doors at ship z -25.7.

The room ends at an internal bulkhead near ship z -21.6. The rest of the level, aft to z -8.8, gets an outer skin with round ports, because the exterior leaves the whole level open (`buildSlavaExterior({ bridgeInterior: true })`). The fittings are the same Soviet 1980s family laid out for the narrower room, and the repeaters hang from the deckhead over the raked front. The eye stands at ship (0.5, 19.0, -30.3) with a 0.4 m lean, over the AK-130 and the Bazalt container fronts. No interior photograph of a Slava-class bridge was found under a usable licence, so the fit and layout are reconstructed; only the shell is measured. 280k triangles and 61 meshes with the crew. `preview/bridge.html?plan=slava` (add `&ship=1` for the exterior and weapons). Without `plan` the Kirov room is unchanged; pixel diffs of nine preview views match exactly.

## Reference-driven Kirov revision

The Kirov exterior and bridge are separate detailed modules, described above.

Manual fire control has acquire, prepare, safe/armed, authorized salvo and abort states, with live track/envelope/ammunition interlocks. Granit/Fort hatch motion, Fort cold ejection/ignition and Osa raise/train are driven by the order. A dedicated weapon camera exposes these mechanics. Automatic SAMs do not take over the manually reserved contact; emergency CIWS still protects the ship.
